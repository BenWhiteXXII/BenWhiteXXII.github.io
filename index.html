The Pale Horse - Book Review

Posted on November 9, 2026

“A lot of it, most of it, I think, is trappings. It’s just to create atmosphere - atmosphere is important. But concealed amongst the trappings, there must be the real thing - the thing that does work.”

The Pale Horse is, to date, the best Christie I’ve read, and that probably has to do with the fact that I’m more of a Chesterton fan than a whodunnit, armchair detective. And this book has all the hallmarks of good Father Brown yarn, even down to an appearance of the vicar’s wife and the bible quotations, all culminating in the swift pull of the curtain, the neutering of evil in the face of reason and a little faith. I picked up the pattern pretty quickly and the build up was entertaining to say the least, though the final twist got me rereading the page looking for typos - Christie does deserve all the praise she gets.

The Earthsea Quartet - Book Review

Posted on November 2, 2026

A series that shaped my childhood and many others, Le Guin’s fairytales are washed in heavy moral greys, vibrant sea blues and brilliant passionate, preadolescent reds. The scenes in these books are like watercolour paintings to me and reveal a depth like no other.

The later books feel more like director’s commentary to me about the world and the lives that these characters live. This might be because I read them at an older age where the lessons weren’t as new and my view of the world was a little more closed. But in any case when I want to get lost in the world of earthsea, the main quartet is the one I’ll come back to time and time again.

Deep Forest

Deep Forest’s self-titled album is a real treat. It’s a relaxing electronic album that blends in world sounds, creating a layered soundscape that blurs the line between ’natural’ and ‘unnatural’ sounds while remaining amazingly listenable. Check it out.

A Quiet Place

Posted on February 10, 2026

Very much enjoyed A Quiet Place by Seicho Matsumoto. What starts as a mystery unravels into a plot reminisent of The Stranger or Crime and Punishment. The ending is a bit sudden and takes the book in a more comic direction. If you like those and are curious about the explosion of Japanese crime novels that’s happening at the moment I recommend checking this one out.

I’m also reading Investigator Imanishi Investigates by the same author, excited to finish it now. Seems that the author is good at laying out meandering plots that arive at unexpected psychological endings.
